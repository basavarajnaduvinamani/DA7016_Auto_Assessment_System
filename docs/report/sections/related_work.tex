\section{Related work}
\subsection{Automated short-answer grading}
Automated short-answer grading predates current generative language models. Mohler et al.\ combine semantic similarity measures and dependency-graph alignments to compare a student response with a reference answer \citep{mohler2011learning}. Their approach makes an enduring distinction: lexical overlap can identify shared words, but correct explanations may use different formulations, while incorrect answers may repeat the reference vocabulary. Auto-Assessment therefore uses a lexical grader as a deliberately limited baseline, not as an adequate substitute for semantic judgement.

Burrows et al.\ survey the eras and trends of automatic short-answer grading \citep{burrows2015eras}. This literature places questions, reference answers and assessment context at the centre of score interpretation. A score predictor that performs well within a familiar question set need not generalise to new questions or grading conventions. The question-disjoint SPRAG split addresses one part of that concern, while remaining narrower than cross-course or cross-institution validation. SPRAG's public release supplies programming-related responses annotated by two graders \citep{sprag2025}; its typed format supports grading evaluation but contributes no handwriting-recognition evidence.

\subsection{Language models as graders and judges}
Kortemeyer studies the pre-trained GPT-4 model on automated short-answer grading \citep{kortemeyer2023grading}. Generative graders can interpret answers without a separately trained classifier for every question, but this flexibility moves substantial responsibility into the prompt, reference material and output checks. Auto-Assessment adds criterion identifiers, bounded awards and verified quotations around that model judgement. These constraints make an award inspectable; they cannot prove that the quoted statement is conceptually correct or that the rubric is complete.

Zheng et al.\ examine LLM-as-a-judge evaluation through MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena, including biases in model judgements \citep{zheng2024judging}. Conversational preference evaluation differs from exam marking: an educational award should follow a criterion rather than general fluency or persuasiveness. Nevertheless, correlated judgement errors and sensitivity to presentation are relevant to both settings. Repeating a call or assigning the same model an advocate and critic role does not create independent human raters. The review ablation in this report tests those roles empirically instead of assuming that an additional judge improves accuracy.

\subsection{Selective prediction and adversarial input}
Selective classification studies the trade-off between answering automatically and abstaining on uncertain cases \citep{geifman2017selective}. Auto-Assessment adopts the operational principle of reporting error together with coverage. Its confidence combines diagnostic signals rather than implementing a calibrated correctness probability or importing a theoretical risk guarantee. An accepted subset must therefore be evaluated under the actual grading configuration; removing the critic also removes a disagreement signal that can trigger referral.

Greshake et al.\ show how instructions embedded in external data can compromise LLM-integrated applications \citep{greshake2023injection}. A student answer is similarly untrusted content crossing a model's instruction boundary. Telling the model that the answer is data is useful but insufficient. The targeted injection measurement illustrates this failure locally. Pattern-based referral is a narrow additional control, not a solution to the broader injection problem described in that work.

\subsection{Indic document and language interfaces}
AI4Bharat's Bodhan IndicOCR model card provides a concrete document-reading integration point \citep{indicocr}. The implementation also uses Bodhan Indic-Translate for feedback while retaining evidence in the answer's original language. Provider interfaces separate recognition and translation from rubric grading, allowing local, precomputed or hosted routes. Advertised language support should not be confused with measured educational validity: this report has a handwritten classroom measurement and a single Hindi worked example, not an Indic-language benchmark. The contribution is the connection between these interfaces, evidence checks and teacher review, rather than a new OCR or translation model.
